Relative severity under weather and surface conditions

Relative risk = severity rate under the condition ÷ rate under clear weather or a dry road, within each dataset. Values below 1 mean less severe. Each source defines severity differently (Canada: fatal; Québec: fatal or serious), so only ratios are compared. Estimates from fewer than 5 events are hidden.

Handling the 1-in-17 imbalance

SVM trained on the same 4,000 severe crashes from 2016–2020, scored on 2021. SMOTE-NC creates synthetic crashes by interpolating between records; with categorical data that underperforms simple class weighting.

Sampling ratio: quality vs. training time

Class-weighted SVM with 3,000 severe crashes and 1–8 non-severe crashes for each one, validated on 2021. Quality barely moves while fit time grows about 19×, so the final model uses 1 : 1.

Hyper-parameter search (RBF kernel)

Mean PR-AUC over 5-fold cross-validation on a 2,000 + 6,000 tuning sample. The best setting is C = 1 with gamma = “scale”; C = 10 over-fits.

Transfer test: train in one region, test in another

What drives each regional model

Permutation importance: the drop in PR-AUC on 2022 crashes when one variable is randomly shuffled (5 repeats; whiskers ±1 SD). Montréal is noisier (189 severe test crashes). Rankings for Montréal and the rest of Québec are essentially unrelated (Spearman ≈ −0.1 for the SVMs).

Direction of effects: odds of a severe outcome

Logistic regression fitted separately per region on all SAAQ years (reference: daylight, clear weather, dry road). Log scale; whiskers are 95% intervals. In Montréal the speed limit has no effect and darkness matters more; elsewhere every +10 km/h raises the odds by about 18%.

Definitions and method
Severe collision
At least one person killed or seriously injured (SAAQ “Mortel ou grave”). Not severe = only minor injuries. Property-damage-only crashes are excluded because they are under-reported.
Year-based split
Models train on 2016–2021 and are tested once on 2022. Design choices (hyper-parameters, imbalance strategy, sampling ratio) were validated on 2021.
Recall
Share of actual severe crashes the model flags.
Precision
Share of flagged crashes that really were severe.
PR-AUC
Average precision across all thresholds. A model with no skill scores the base rate (6.2% in 2022); lift divides by that rate.
Balanced accuracy
Average of recall on severe and on non-severe crashes; 0.5 means no skill.
Relative risk
Severity rate under a condition divided by the rate under the reference condition in the same dataset.
Limitations
No impact speed, seatbelt use, alcohol, age or traffic volume in open data. Severity and conditions are recorded by police at the scene. The 2018 SAAQ file is missing. Montréal has few severe test crashes.
